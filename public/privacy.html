Who runs Thriftly

Thriftly is a small app for people reselling secondhand items: you photograph something, the software identifies it, suggests a price and drafts listings for resale platforms. The operator is the person or entity that hosts the copy of Thriftly you're signing up on.

If you upload a document rather than a photo — a PDF, a Word, PowerPoint or Excel file, an OpenDocument file, an EPUB or a zip — Thriftly looks for the image inside it, and that image is what gets stored and sent to Google. The document itself is processed in memory and never written to storage.

What happens to your photos and text at Google

Plainly: when you scan an item or generate a listing, the photo you uploaded (or the image taken from the document you uploaded) and the text around it — your notes, the instructions sent to the model, and the listings it writes back — go to Google's Gemini API on your own API key. From that point Google handles them under its terms, not this page's, and on the free tier those terms are broad.

If your key is on Gemini's free tier — the default, with no billing enabled — Google's terms say it uses the content you submit (your photos and text) and the responses it generates to provide, improve and develop Google products and services and machine learning technologies. Google also states that human reviewers may read, annotate and process that input and output to improve quality, with the data disconnected from your account and API key before they see it. Google's own advice for that tier is blunt: don't submit sensitive, confidential or personal information.

In practice, for a reselling app, that means:

  • Assume a photo you scan may be looked at by a person at Google, and may contribute to improving a model.
  • Photograph the item. Keep faces, ID documents, post, screens, number plates and anything showing where you live out of frame — crop or reshoot rather than upload and hope.
  • Don't type anything into item notes you wouldn't want read: names, addresses, phone numbers, gate codes, buyer details.

If that isn't acceptable to you, don't scan. Two things change it: a paid Gemini key, where Google's terms say prompts, images and responses are not used to improve its products and are logged only briefly to detect abuse — and, regardless of tier, accounts in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, where Google applies those paid-tier data terms even to the free service. Thriftly can't choose your tier or alter how Google handles what it receives; it sends only what the scan or listing needs. Everything sent to Google is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and the Gemini API Additional Terms, which override anything on this page.

Deleting a draft, or your whole account, removes the photo from Thriftly — but not from Google. Once content has been submitted on the free tier there is no way for us to recall it, because we never had a copy of what Google did with it.

What we don't collect

  • No analytics, advertising or tracking of any kind.
  • No payment information — the service costs nothing to run for you, and you bring your own Gemini key.
  • No contacts, location data, or anything off the one photo you choose to scan.

Third parties involved

  • Google (Gemini API). When you scan a photo or generate listings, your photo and the item text are sent to Google's Gemini service using your own API key, to identify the item and draft copy. This is a direct consequence of using the feature. On the free tier, Google may use those photos and that text to improve its models, and human reviewers may read them — see What happens to your photos and text at Google above for the details and what to keep out of frame.
  • Email provider. Verification and password-reset emails are delivered through the operator's SMTP provider, which processes the recipient address and message content to send them.
  • Cloudflare (Turnstile). If the operator has enabled the signup CAPTCHA, the signup page loads a Turnstile challenge from Cloudflare, which processes your IP address and browser signals to judge whether the request is human; the operator's server then verifies the resulting token with Cloudflare. The app itself stores none of that challenge data.
  • Hosting provider. The server (database, files, code) runs on cloud infrastructure the operator rents, which may include a managed database and file-storage provider (for example Supabase) for the database and the photos.

How long we keep things

Your account, drafts and photos are kept until you delete them. Verification and password-reset tokens are single-use, expire quickly, and are purged on a schedule. Deleting a draft removes its photo. Deleting your account (see below) removes everything at once from the live service.

Backups are different, and they cover less than people usually assume. Thriftly keeps no archive of its own on the server; the database is hosted by Supabase, and whether it is backed up automatically depends on the plan the operator chose. Supabase's free plan includes no automatic backups — on that plan, if it is ever lost, it is lost, unless the operator has exported their own copies. Paid plans keep daily database backups for 7 days (Pro), 14 (Team) or up to 30 (Enterprise), restorable inside Supabase but not downloadable. Where those backups exist, a deleted account can persist in one for up to that window; a backup is only ever restored to recover from a failure, never to bring back data you asked us to delete.

Your photos are in no backup at all, on any plan — Supabase's database backups don't include Storage objects. A photo exists only where you see it, so deleting a draft or your account removes it permanently; there is no archive to restore it from. The same goes for what was sent to Google (see above).

Your rights and choices

  • Delete your account: while signed in, "Delete account…" erases your account, every draft, every photo, and all sessions immediately and irreversibly.
  • Disconnect your Gemini key: one button in settings removes it; the encrypted copy is deleted.
  • Delete drafts: individually or all at once, removes the draft and its photo.
  • Session control: logging out invalidates the current session; changing your password (via password reset) logs out every device.
  • Export: "Download my data" gives you one JSON file with your account, every draft, and every photo inline, so your data leaves with you.

Security

Passwords are hashed with bcrypt; your Gemini key is encrypted at rest; sessions are bearer tokens that only work over the app's API; and the app sets standard browser protections (content-security policy, no-sniff, etc.). No system is perfectly secure, and the operator's server is the trust anchor — choose an operator you trust.

Children

Thriftly isn't intended for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.
